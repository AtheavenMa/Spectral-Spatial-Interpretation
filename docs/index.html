Which spectral channels matter, and where?

HAOTIAN MANEURIPS 2026
~25 s

Talk track

A satellite image contains information in several spectral bands. A spatial heatmap can show where a model responds, while a spectral ranking can tell us which bands matter overall. What we really want is their joint answer. In this five-minute tutorial, we follow one actual burn-scar image through the SSI method and then look at the evaluation reported in the paper.

这一教程从真实的六波段烧毁区域图像出发，解释 SSI 如何在同一计算中保留位置和通道两个维度。

Our running example is one 128-by-128 HLS burn-scar image. The U-Net receives the original six-band input and predicts burn-scar logits. To produce one scalar score for explanation, we average the burn-class logit over a fixed ground-truth burn region. That mask defines the measurement but is never an extra model input. A global spectral ranking tells us whether SWIR2 or Blue matters overall. A spatial heatmap tells us which part of the image matters overall. Neither separates the effect of one spectral band at one particular location. SSI keeps both dimensions.

One matrix, not two explainers

The joint attribution Phi(g,c) is a 16×16×6 tensor for this run: 256 locations and six channels. A row gives the six within-cell Shapley contributions. Fixing a channel shows how its contribution varies across locations.

Every small map here comes directly from the real 1,536-element attribution tensor, with one common diverging signed color scale. The thin colored band markers encode channel identity; red and teal within each map encode positive and negative attribution, separately from band identity.

Talk track

We now repeat the same procedure at all 256 spatial locations. Each location yields six signed numbers, so the full result is one 256-by-6 joint attribution object. These six maps are not six separate explainers; they are the six slices of that one tensor. The identity color above each map tells us which band we are looking at, while red versus teal shows positive versus negative model attribution. Click a map to inspect its global signed total. Keeping this joint structure is what makes the next two summaries consistent.

Exact aggregation of the same tensor

Spatial: S(g) = sum over c of Phi(g,c). Spectral: T(c) = sum over g of Phi(g,c). The six per-channel spatial maps are the fixed-channel slices of the same tensor.

Local efficiency is exact within each spatial cell: S(g) = f(x) - f(x with all channels of g masked). However, summing S(g) over cells need not recover the global score f(x) or a whole-image baseline contrast. For this run the sum across cells/channels is 0.874582, while the full target logit is 1.206524.

~45 s

Talk track

Now there is no need to run a spatial method and a spectral method separately. If we sum each location's six values, we obtain the spatial map: where the evidence is. If we sum the contribution of each band over all locations, we obtain the global spectral chart: which bands support or oppose the chosen model score. Both are views of the same tensor. In this example, SWIR2 and SWIR1 have the largest positive global contributions, while Red is negative. These are signed effects on a logit, not reflectance or burn probabilities. The exact accounting guarantee is local to each cell, not a decomposition of the entire model score.

空间归因是对六个通道求和；全局光谱归因是对所有位置求和。两者来自同一个张量。不能将所有 SSI 值的总和误称为模型最终 logit。

What the evidence supports

For the single HLS sample, the SWIR1 and SWIR2 signed spatial maps have Pearson correlation −0.5318 even though each band has a positive global total. At cell A, SWIR2 is +0.0730 while SWIR1 is −0.0313. These are observations for a fixed score target, not a general theorem about burn spectroscopy.

Separately, the paper reports the best mean among compared methods on joint deletion, rank-1 deletion and insertion for CUB-200, EuroSAT and HLS Burn Scars with mean and zero baselines. The displayed joint-deletion values are from the mean-baseline Table 1, not the training-split sample shown earlier.

Here is why the joint answer matters. In our real HLS example, the two short-wave infrared bands have positive total contributions, but their spatial contribution maps are negatively correlated. At a particular cell one is positive and the other is negative. A single spatial map or global spectral ranking would lose that distinction. The paper also evaluates whether the joint ranking is useful under deletion and insertion protocols. The three datasets shown here each have a lower, better mean joint-deletion score than the best competing method. This case illustrates the method; the benchmark results come from separate paper experiments. Explore the live Playground or read the paper for the full evaluation.
